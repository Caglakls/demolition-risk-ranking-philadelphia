The header paragraph
"Raw pooled PR-AUC mixes six separately trained models whose score scales are not comparable"

Rolling-origin doesn't fit one model. It fits six — train 2015–18 → test 2019, train 2015–19 → test 2020, and so on. Each produces probabilities for its own test year.

When you pool all six years of predictions into one metric, you're mixing scores from six different models. Model A might centre its scores around 0.3 and Model B around 0.6, purely because they saw different training data. Pooling then partly measures score-scale drift between models rather than ranking quality.

The fix is to convert each year's scores to a percentile within that year — parcel ranked 1st of 526,867, 2nd, 3rd — before pooling. That's on a common 0–100 scale regardless of what each model's raw numbers looked like. It's also what you actually do downstream: rank parcels within a year and take the top slice. This was §11.5 of your Fishtown notes; I made it the default sort rather than a post-hoc check.

Here the two agree closely (0.0745 vs 0.0733), which is itself reassuring — it means the score scales didn't drift much.

The metrics
PR-AUC 0.0733 — precision-recall area. Looks tiny, and that's expected: at a 0.077% event rate, random guessing scores about 0.00077. Never report this number without its baseline.

Lift over prevalence 94.75 — the honest version of the above. The model ranks ~95× better than chance. Fishtown's combined model was 15×.

ROC-AUC 0.9450 — pick one demolished parcel and one surviving parcel at random; the model ranks the demolished one higher 94.5% of the time. This is the number you compare to your old 0.745, and it's the direct answer to your earlier question about getting past 0.80.

Top-10% capture 0.841 — score every parcel, take the highest-risk 10%, and that group contains 84% of all demolitions that actually happened. Fishtown was 38%. This is the metric that matters most for your MFA, because your workflow is exactly "take the top N and compute their materials."

Top-20% capture 0.907 — widening to 20% gets you 91%. Note the diminishing return: doubling the group adds only 7 points. That says the risk is concentrated, which is good for you — you can work with a small slice.

Yearly PR-AUC sd 0.013398 — the spread across the six test years. Small relative to the mean of 0.073, meaning no single year is carrying the result. This is the metric I added specifically because of Fishtown, where the 2021 fold (7 events, PR-AUC 0.189) dominated everything.

The five checks — and why my recommendation differs from this file
The script deliberately prints the numerically top line and then refuses to call it the winner. Working through the checks is what produced my different recommendation:

Check	Result
1. Yearly stability	Passes. ROC-AUC 0.924–0.960 across all six folds.
2. Does combined_extended beat combined_compact?	Yes — 0.0733 vs 0.0607 PR-AUC, and three of the top four SHAP features are new ones. The added features earn their place.
3. Does the gain survive combined_strict?	Yes, almost entirely — 0.0697 vs 0.0733, ROC 0.9429 vs 0.9450, top-10% 84.0% vs 84.1%.
4. Coefficient and SHAP plausibility	SHAP is coherent. Logistic has a collinearity problem — use it for direction only.
5. Logistic–XGBoost overlap	Spearman 0.69, 56% top-decile overlap. Carry into MFA sensitivity.
Check 3 is why I recommended combined_strict over the line this file prints. Dropping new_construction, zoning_use and expired — the three features that either sit too close to an already-made redevelopment decision or aren't time-safe at all — costs 0.002 in ROC-AUC. That's nothing, and it buys you a model where nobody can argue the performance came from features that were quietly reading the outcome.

The file names combined_extended because it sorts on one number. You have the other four checks, so you can make the better call.

The final warning
"Do not report a score of 0.7 as a 70% chance"

XGBoost was trained with scale_pos_weight, which deliberately reweights the rare positive class so the model can learn from 5,249 events among 5.3M rows. That reweighting inflates the output numbers. A parcel scoring 0.70 is near the top of the ranking, not 70% likely to be demolished — the true annual rate for even the highest-risk parcels is a few percent.

This matters concretely for your MFA: do not sum raw scores to estimate how many buildings will be demolished. It would overstate the count enormously. Use the ranking plus explicit low/medium/high scenarios, as §16 of your Fishtown notes already sets out.
